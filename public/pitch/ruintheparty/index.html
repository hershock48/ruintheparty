One decision we made that you can overrule.

Your brief says the merchandise should not feel like nonprofit merchandise, and it is right. Section two

How it is built, and what you own.

When the build fee is paid in full, these are yours: the design, the source code, every word on it, the accounts, the code repository, the hosting project, and every login to them. The domain stays registered in your name and transfers to any registrar you name, free, whenever you ask. After launch it is month to month, thirty days' notice, no penalty, and the site keeps working on the way out the door. The terms are one plain-English page at glazedweb.com/agreement, the same page every client gets.

Nothing underneath the site is rented. No store platform, no page builder, no form service, no plugin bill arriving in a year. The one exception is Stripe, because somebody has to take the card: its fee is published, it is charged to your account, and it is the only line on any bill that is not ours.

Each one: what to say, what to do, when to get somebody with authority, and the next day.

Parents. Your nine topics, in your order, each with the first sentence a parent could actually say in a car.

Teams. A four-minute locker-room talk a coach can give before a party weekend, the season program that has evidence behind it, and a form that asks coaches and schools what they need. Section four

The store.

The buy button on every product goes to a checkout page hosted by Stripe, on your Stripe account, not ours. Prices resolve on the server from one file, so a tampered order cannot invent one. The money settles to the bank account you name, on Stripe's schedule, at Stripe's published rate.

It is off today, behind a single setting, because a buy button put in front of a visitor promises a store nobody has stocked. Nothing can be charged on the site as it stands. Press Buy and it tells you so, in those words.

The one thing to decide before it switches on

Who prints it, and who ships it.

Two ways this works. You hold stock and ship it yourself, in which case the site tells you an order came in and you put it in a box. Or a print-on-demand service makes and ships each piece and takes its cut, in which case the site hands the order across and you never touch a box. The site is built for either, the price of the build does not move, and the agreement names which one.

What decides it is how many you expect to sell in the first year, and neither of us knows yet. It is a short conversation.

Prices. The five on the demo are placeholders so the shop reads as a shop. Yours go in one file, and the page stops calling them samples the day you set them.

What it costs to run. Stripe's rate is Stripe's, charged to your account; we do not touch it and we do not mark it up. On top of that there is nothing: no percentage of the ticket, no per-order fee, no platform subscription.

We are a website studio, not a clothing label. The products on the demo are our best reading of your boards. The blanks, the print method and the patch on the hat are decisions you will make with whoever prints them, and the site will show whatever you decide.

$150/mo
Hosting, SSL, security updates, backups, domain renewal where we hold it, the Stripe order notifications, and edits. A new product, a new scenario, a changed number on the resources page? Send a text.
